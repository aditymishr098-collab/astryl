The Andromeda Galaxy

The nearest big galaxy, and the one that will one day merge with ours.

~2.5 million light-yearsDistance
about 1 trillionStars
~150,000 light-yearsWidth of the disk
Messier 31, M31Catalogue names

Andromeda is a spiral galaxy in the constellation Andromeda. It is larger than the Milky Way and has more stars, though the Milky Way may hold more dark matter. It is the farthest thing most people can see with the naked eye.

Light from the past

Its light takes about 2.5 million years to reach us. When you see Andromeda tonight, you see it as it was when early humans were just appearing on Earth.

A collision is coming

Andromeda is moving toward us at about 110 km per second. If the two galaxies merge, they will settle into one big elliptical galaxy that people sometimes call Milkomeda.

Stars are so far apart that almost none will actually hit another. The Sun will probably be thrown into a new orbit, but not destroyed by the collision. By then the Sun will already be brighter and Earth far too hot for life as we know it.

Earlier studies expected a sure collision in about 4 to 5 billion years. A 2025 study that also counted the Large Magellanic Cloud and other galaxies puts the chance of a merger within 10 billion years at roughly one in two, most likely in about 8 billion years. A merger within the next 5 billion years now looks unlikely.

How to find it

On a dark autumn evening in the northern sky, find the Great Square of Pegasus and follow the line of stars of Andromeda. A faint, elongated smudge appears beside the star ν Andromedae. Binoculars show it much better.
