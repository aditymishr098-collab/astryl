The Local Group and beyond

Galaxies come in groups, groups in clusters, clusters in giant threads.

Our neighbourhood

The Milky Way belongs to the Local Group, about 10 million light-years across, with more than 80 known galaxies. Two giants dominate it: the Milky Way and Andromeda. Most of the rest are dwarfs that orbit one of the two.

How far is far?

Voyager 1, the fastest spacecraft we have sent out of the Solar System, moves at about 17 km per second. At that speed:

TripDistanceTime at Voyager speed
Proxima Centauri, the nearest star4.24 light-years74,772 years
The centre of the Milky Way26,000 light-years459 million years
Large Magellanic Cloud160,000 light-years3 billion years
Andromeda Galaxy2,500,000 light-years44 billion years

Worked out from distance ÷ 17 km/s. Even the nearest star would take about 75,000 years.

Clusters and the cosmic web

The nearest big cluster of galaxies, the Virgo Cluster, is about 54 million light-years away and holds over a thousand galaxies. Clusters join into superclusters. Our home supercluster, Laniakea, spans about 500 million light-years and holds roughly 100,000 galaxies.

On the largest scales galaxies line up in filaments around huge empty voids, like the walls of soap bubbles. This pattern is called the cosmic web.
