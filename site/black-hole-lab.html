Black hole lab

Bend the sky, size any black hole and slow down a clock. It all runs in your browser and nothing is sent anywhere.

1. Bend the sky

Gravity bends light, so a black hole in front of stars acts like a lens. Drag the black hole across the sky and watch the stars behind it stretch into arcs and a bright ring, called the Einstein ring. Use the slider to make it heavier.

A point-lens model, not a full ray-traced image: real black holes also show an accretion disk and the photon ring. The orange circle marks the Einstein radius.

2. How big is it?

Type any mass and get the size of its event horizon, r = 2GM/c², plus a few more things worked out from it. Tap a preset to try an example.

Density is the mass divided by the volume inside the horizon. Hawking temperature and evaporation time come from Hawking’s 1974 formulas; they are predictions, never measured.

3. Slow down a clock

Near a black hole, time runs slower as seen from far away. Pick a mass and move the slider to set how close you hover, measured in horizon radii. At 1 you are at the event horizon.

For a clock that hovers at rest at that distance. The clock rate is √(1 − r_s/r). It depends only on the distance in horizon radii, so a bigger black hole gives the same effect farther out in km.
