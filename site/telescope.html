🔭 Telescope eyepiece simulator

Pick a telescope and an eyepiece to see the magnification, how much sky fits in view and roughly what the Moon, planets and deep-sky objects look like.

Real photos (NASA, Hubble, and Adam Evans for Andromeda), shown at their true size for this telescope. Real views are fainter and have less colour, and many telescopes show the view upside down. Planets look tiny even at high power; detail comes with practice and steady air.

How the numbers work

  • Magnification = telescope focal length ÷ eyepiece focal length (× 2 with a Barlow).
  • True field of view = the eyepiece’s apparent field ÷ magnification. The full Moon is about half a degree wide.
  • Exit pupil = aperture ÷ magnification. Between about 1 and 7 mm is useful.
  • Highest useful magnification ≈ 2 × aperture in mm; above that the view only gets blurrier.
  • Faintest star ≈ 2.7 + 5 × log₁₀(aperture in mm), under a dark sky. Finest detail ≈ 116 ÷ aperture in mm, in arcseconds (Dawes limit).
⚠️ Never point a telescope or binoculars at the Sun without a proper solar filter over the front. It can blind you instantly.
