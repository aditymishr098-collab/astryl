Universe map

One 3D space with the planets where they are right now, nearby stars, 62 black holes and 3,600 real galaxies. Drag to turn, pinch or scroll to zoom, tap anything.

What is real here

The planets are where they are at this moment, from the orbit formulas of NASA JPL. Stars, black holes and galaxies sit at their real directions and distances. Sizes and shapes of galaxies come from the catalogue, and a black hole’s horizon is drawn at its real size when you zoom in on it.

What is a model

The dots of the Milky Way are a model with the real size, a bar and four arms: nobody can map single stars that far. Distances of far galaxies are estimated from their redshift, so some groups look stretched along our line of sight. The catalogue holds the brightest galaxies, most of them within 500 million light-years; the real universe has hundreds of billions, perhaps two trillion.

Speeding up time

In real time nothing here seems to move: the Sun needs about 230 million years for one trip around the Galaxy. The speed buttons run the clock faster so you can watch planets orbit and the Milky Way turn. The disk is turned as one piece to keep the picture simple.
