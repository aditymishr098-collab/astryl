Where are the spacecraft now?

Voyager 1 is a full light-day from home. See where it and seven other spacecraft are right now, how fast they move and how long their signals take.

Distances are estimates. For the far probes they are worked out from average speed and can be off by about 1%, because Earth moves round the Sun. The Mars distance is calculated for today. One AU (astronomical unit) is the distance from Earth to the Sun.

Where our spacecraft are

Eight spacecraft are shown here, from the space station a few hundred kilometres up to Voyager 1, the most distant object people have ever built. Each card shows how far away it is today, how fast it is moving and how long a radio signal takes to reach it.

Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 were launched in 1977. Both have left the bubble of the Sun’s wind and are now in interstellar space. New Horizons flew past Pluto in 2015 and is still heading outwards. The James Webb Space Telescope works about 1.5 million kilometres from Earth, four times farther than the Moon.

Radio signals travel at the speed of light, yet a message to Voyager 1 still takes almost a whole day to arrive, and the answer another day to come back.

Questions

Which spacecraft is farthest from Earth?

Voyager 1. It is about a light-day away, more than 25 billion kilometres, and moves about 17 km every second.

Are the Voyagers still working?

Yes, they still send data, but their power is running down, so engineers have switched off some instruments to keep the rest going for as long as possible.

How is the distance worked out?

For the far probes it is worked out from their known speed and direction, so it can be off by about 1% as Earth moves round the Sun. The Mars distance is calculated for today.
