Geminids meteor shower

Peaks every year around 14 December, with up to 150 meteors an hour under a dark sky.

Key facts

14 DecPeak night
150Meteors an hour (best case)
35 km/sSpeed

Where it comes from

Earth passes through the dust left behind by the asteroid 3200 Phaethon. Each grain, often smaller than a grain of sand, burns up about 100 km above us as a shooting star.

The meteors seem to fly out of one point in the sky, the radiant, in Gemini, near Castor. You do not need to look there: meteors appear all over the sky.

When and how to watch

All night from about 10 pm, best around 2 am, from almost anywhere.

  • Get away from city lights. A dark country sky can show several times as many meteors as a city sky.
  • Lie back and look at a wide patch of sky. No telescope or binoculars needed.
  • Give your eyes 20 minutes to get used to the dark, and keep your phone screen off.
  • A bright Moon washes out the faint meteors: check the Moon on the night.
The richest shower of the year, and one of the few that comes from an asteroid rather than a comet.
