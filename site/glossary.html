Space glossary

49 words you meet when reading about the universe, explained simply.

Accretion disk

A flat whirl of hot gas that circles a black hole or a star before falling in.

Astronomical unit (AU)

The average distance from Earth to the Sun, about 150 million km.

Big Bang

The hot, dense beginning of the observable universe about 13.8 billion years ago, from which space has been expanding.

Binary star

Two stars that orbit each other.

Black hole

A region where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape from inside its event horizon.

Brown dwarf

An object between a planet and a star, below about 8% of the Sun’s mass, too small for hydrogen fusion.

Chandrasekhar limit

About 1.4 times the Sun’s mass: the most a white dwarf can weigh. Worked out by Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar.

Cosmic microwave background

The faint glow left from when the universe was 380,000 years old. It fills the whole sky.

Cosmic web

The largest structure of the universe: galaxies arranged in filaments around huge empty voids.

Dark energy

The unknown cause that makes the expansion of the universe speed up. It makes up about 68% of the universe’s total energy.

Dark matter

Invisible matter that we detect only through its gravity. It makes up about 27% of the universe.

Dwarf galaxy

A small galaxy with up to a few billion stars. The most common kind.

Event horizon

The boundary around a black hole. Nothing that crosses it can return.

Exoplanet

A planet that orbits a star other than the Sun.

Galaxy

A huge system of stars, gas, dust and dark matter held together by gravity.

Gravitational lensing

The bending of light by gravity, which can magnify or distort objects behind a massive body.

Gravitational waves

Ripples in space itself, made for example when two black holes merge. First detected in 2015.

Hawking radiation

A faint glow that quantum effects should make a black hole give off. Predicted in 1974 but never observed.

HR diagram

A chart of stars by surface temperature and brightness. Most stars fall on a band called the main sequence.

Hubble constant

How fast the universe expands: about 70 km per second for every megaparsec of distance. The exact value is still debated.

Infrared

Light with a longer wavelength than red that our eyes cannot see. It can pass through dust.

Intermediate-mass black hole

A black hole between about 100 and 100,000 Suns. Few have been found.

Jet

A narrow beam of gas shot out at nearly the speed of light along the spin axis of a black hole or young star.

Light-year

The distance light travels in one year, about 9.46 trillion km.

Local Group

The group of more than 80 galaxies that includes the Milky Way and Andromeda.

Main sequence

The long, steady part of a star’s life when it fuses hydrogen into helium in its core. The Sun is on it now.

Nebula

A cloud of gas and dust in space, where stars may be born or where a dying star has thrown off its layers.

Neutron star

The crushed core of a massive star, about 20 km wide yet heavier than the Sun.

Nuclear fusion

Light atomic nuclei joining into heavier ones and giving off energy. It is what makes stars shine.

Observable universe

The part of the universe whose light has had time to reach us, about 93 billion light-years across.

Parsec

A unit of distance used by astronomers: about 3.26 light-years.

Photon sphere

The distance, 1.5 times the horizon radius for a non-spinning black hole, where light can orbit in a circle.

Planetary nebula

The glowing shell of gas a Sun-like star blows off at the end of its life. Nothing to do with planets.

Protostar

A young star still gathering gas, shining from the heat of collapse before fusion starts.

Pulsar

A spinning neutron star that sends out beams of radio waves, seen as regular pulses.

Quasar

The extremely bright core of a distant galaxy, powered by gas falling into a supermassive black hole.

Red giant

A star near the end of its life that has swollen to many times its size and cooled to red. The Sun will become one.

Redshift

The stretching of light to longer, redder wavelengths, caused by motion away from us, the expansion of space or strong gravity.

Schwarzschild radius

The size of the event horizon of a non-spinning black hole: r = 2GM/c², about 3 km per Sun of mass.

Singularity

The centre of a black hole, where our theories say density becomes infinite. It is where they stop working.

Solar mass

The mass of the Sun, about 2 × 10³⁰ kg. Black holes and stars are measured in solar masses.

Spaghettification

The stretching of an object into a long thin shape by the difference in gravity across it near a black hole.

Spectral class

A star’s type by surface temperature: O, B, A, F, G, K, M, from hottest blue to coolest red. The Sun is G.

Spiral galaxy

A galaxy with a flat disk and curving arms. The Milky Way is one.

Supermassive black hole

A black hole of millions to billions of Suns, found in the centre of most large galaxies.

Supernova

The explosion of a massive star at the end of its life, or of a white dwarf that has taken in too much matter.

Void

A huge region of space with very few galaxies, tens to hundreds of millions of light-years across.

White dwarf

The glowing core left by a Sun-like star after it ends its life. About the size of Earth.

Wormhole

A hypothetical tunnel through space and time that appears in the equations of relativity. None has ever been found.
