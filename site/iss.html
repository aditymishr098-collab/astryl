🛰️ When can I see the space station?

The International Space Station (ISS) is the brightest thing in the night sky after the Moon and Venus. Here is where it is right now and when it flies over your city, worked out in your browser from its real orbit.

⏳ Loading…

How to see it

  • Look for a steady, bright white “star” moving smoothly across the sky. It does not blink; aeroplanes do.
  • A pass lasts a few minutes. Be outside a couple of minutes early and face the direction it comes from.
  • The higher the pass (in degrees), the brighter it is. 90° is straight overhead; 10° is just above the horizon.
  • It often fades out suddenly in the middle of the sky. That is the moment it flies into Earth’s shadow.

Why only some passes are visible

The station has no lights you could see from the ground; it shines because sunlight reflects off it. So you can only see it when your sky is dark (after sunset or before sunrise) but the station, 400 km up, is still in sunlight. In the middle of the night it is usually in Earth’s shadow, and in the day the sky is too bright.

It circles Earth about every 92 minutes, about 16 times a day, at around 27,600 km/h.

Orbit data: wheretheiss.at and CelesTrak, kept by Astryl’s server for a few hours. Positions use SGP4, the standard model for satellite orbits.
