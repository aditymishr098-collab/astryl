Privacy Policy

Last updated 17 November 2026 · applies to astryl.space

In plain words: we do not sell anything about you, there are no ads, and you can use everything without an account. This page lists exactly what is kept when you do choose something, and how to remove it.

Our promises

🚫

No ads, nothing sold

No ad networks, no data brokers, no third-party analytics.

👤

Account is optional, no e-mail

Just a username, a name to show and a password. No e-mail or phone number.

📊

Counted, not tracked

Page views are only added up: no cookie, no IP address, no id.

🗑️

You can delete it all

One button deletes your account and everything stored for it.

1. Who is responsible

Astryl (astryl.space) is made by Adity Mishra. For questions about your data, write to contact@astryl.space.

2. In your browser

  • Planet facts, the Moon, planet positions, eclipses, the star map and the sky for your city are worked out in your browser. Your city is never sent for this.
  • Your browser remembers your theme, city, quiz scores, saved things, your “days in a row” count and whether you have seen the welcome window, in local storage on your own device. If you log in, a login token is kept there too, until you log out.
  • To work offline and open fast, the website keeps copies of its own pages, scripts and pictures in your browser (a service worker cache). It stores nothing about you and sends nothing. Clearing the site data in your browser removes all of this.
  • No cookies, no ads, no third-party analytics, scripts or fonts.

3. The account

The account is optional: you can read and use the website without one. It keeps your favourites, scores and settings on every device, and lets you take part in the community and the leaderboards. It needs a username, a name to show (it does not have to be your real name) and a password. No e-mail address or phone number is asked for.

4. What every account does

These come with the account and cannot be switched off one by one. If you do not want them, you can use the website without an account, or delete your account.

  • Your name is public. The name you choose and your username are shown on what you share, on the leaderboards (with your score, right answers and time) and on your profile page. People who chose to be hidden from the leaderboard before 17 November 2026 stay hidden.
  • You are counted as an active user. Your browser sends a random number it made itself, at most once every few hours, with no username. It is not linked to you or your account, is kept for 90 days and is used only to show how many people use Astryl.
  • Your country is counted. The country from your browser’s language and region setting is kept with that random number and shown only as a count per country. It is not worked out from your IP address or your location.

5. Comments and reports without an account

  • Blog comments need no account. We store the name you type (or “Guest”), your comment, the time, a scrambled form (hash) of a random id kept in your browser, so you can delete your own comment, and a scrambled form of your internet address that changes every day, used only to limit how often one person can post. Your internet address itself is not stored. Comments are public; reported ones are checked by the moderator.
  • Reporting a photo, post or comment needs no account. To count each person once, the browser keeps a random id; the server stores only a scrambled form of it with the report.

6. Counting visits

To see which pages are read, each page view adds one to a daily total on the Astryl server. This is what is counted, and nothing else:

CountedNot stored at all
The page name, the language, a size class of your screen (small, medium or large), the website that sent you here (if any), and the country.Your IP address itself, a cookie, any id, the time of day, your browser or device model, or anything that could point to one person.

The country is read by the network from the connection when the request arrives; the address itself is not kept. Rows are kept for 400 days. Nothing is counted if your browser sends “Do Not Track” or “Global Privacy Control”.

To see how many different people visit, the server makes a one-way code from your IP address, the day and a secret that only the server knows, and adds that code to the list for the day. The IP address itself is not saved. The code cannot be turned back into an address and is different every day, so a person cannot be followed from one day to the next. The codes are deleted after 90 days. This is not done if your browser sends “Do Not Track” or “Global Privacy Control”.

A small notice can ask, once, whether you want to share your approximate place. It is optional. If you press “Share”, your browser asks for permission; the point is rounded to about 10 km and added to a count for the day, with no code, no account and no IP address. If you say no, nothing is sent.

7. Where the data is kept and who handles it

Account data is stored with Supabase (database and login service), on servers in Singapore. The website reaches it only through server functions that check who is asking; the tables themselves cannot be read from outside. To limit abuse, a hashed (not readable) key and a counter are kept for up to 2 days; no IP address is stored in readable form. The website itself is a plain static site served by GitHub Pages, which follows GitHub’s own privacy statement.

The home page shows NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day: the text comes through the Astryl server and the picture loads straight from NASA’s server, which sees your IP address like any website. The space station page gets the station’s orbit data from the Astryl server (or, if that fails, from wheretheiss.at). Nothing about you is sent.

We do not sell data, show ads, use third-party analytics or trackers, or share data with anyone else.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Delete your account at any time on the Account page. Your account and everything stored for it online are removed straight away.
  • Change your name at any time, and your username once every 30 days.
  • Log out to stop syncing on a device.
  • To ask what is stored about you, to get a copy, or to have something corrected or removed, write to contact@astryl.space and tell us your username. Depending on where you live you may also have the right to complain to your data protection authority.

A short version of all this is on the Your data page.

9. Children

Astryl is for everyone who likes space. If you are under 16, please ask a parent or guardian before making an account. The website is fully usable without one.

10. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the new version is published on this page with a new date. The full source code, including the server code in the folder supabase/, is open for anyone to read on GitHub.
