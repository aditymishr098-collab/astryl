Privacy Policy

Last updated 12 November 2026 · applies to astryl.space, and to the old Astryl Android app for people who still use it (it gets no new versions; 1.40.0 was the last)

In plain words: we do not sell anything about you, there are no ads, and you can use everything without an account. This page lists exactly what is kept when you do choose something, and how to remove it.

🚫

No ads, nothing sold

No ad networks, no data brokers, no third-party analytics.

👤

One account, no e-mail

The app needs an account; the website does not. Just a username and a password, no e-mail, name or phone number.

📊

Counted, not tracked

Page views are only added up: no cookie, no IP address, no id.

🗑️

You can delete it all

One button deletes your account and everything stored for it.

1. Who is responsible

For questions about your data, write to contact@astryl.space.

2. On your phone or in your browser

  • Planet facts, the Moon, planet positions, eclipses, the star map and the quiz are worked out on your phone or in your browser.
  • Your city, birth date, favourites, quiz scores and settings are saved on your phone. The Backup file is made by you and goes only where you send it.
  • Update check: each time the app opens, it asks GitHub for the newest release and, if there is one, offers it first (you can tap Skip). GitHub sees your IP address, like in any web request.
  • Astryl news (on by default, switch it off in Settings): about every six hours the app asks the Astryl server "anything new?" and shows a notification when the team has published a note. Only that question is sent, with nothing about you, and nothing is stored about the check.
  • Live features are always on. The app downloads orbit data, the rocket launch list, space news and NASA’s space photo of the day from public services (wheretheiss.at, Celestrak, Launch Library 2, Spaceflight News API, NASA through the Astryl server), which can see your IP address. Nothing about you is sent.
  • “Use my location” in the city list asks Android for your approximate location, once, and uses it only on your phone to work out the sky. Nothing is sent for this.

3. The account

The account is for one thing: keeping your favourites, scores and settings in step between the app and this website. It needs a username and a password. No e-mail address, name or phone number is asked for. The app asks you to make an account (or log in) when it is first opened. On the website the account is optional: “Continue without account” lets you look around.

4. Switches (you can change each one in your account)

  • Count me as an active user: the app sends a random number made on your phone, at most once every few hours, with no username. It is not linked to you or your account. It is kept for 90 days and is used only to show how many people use Astryl.
  • Share my country: the country from your phone's (or browser's) language and region setting, kept with that random number and shown only as a count per country. It is not worked out from your IP address or your location.
  • Share my approximate location (app only, needs an account): when you make an account, the app asks Android for the location permission. If you allow it, this switch is on and the app sends Android's approximate location (a few kilometres) when you open it, at most once a day. The server rounds it to about 10 km and keeps only the latest place with its date, tied to your account. There is no location in the background. Switch it off in your account and the place is deleted from the server. If you say no to the permission, nothing is sent.
  • Show me on the leaderboard (on by default): the Daily 5 leaderboard shows your username, score, right answers and time. Switch it off in your account (website or the You tab in the app) and your name no longer appears; your own rank stays visible to you.

5. Crash reports (on by default, you can switch them off)

  • If the app crashes, it sends one small report to the Astryl server: the error text, the technical trace of where it happened, the app version, the Android version string and the app language. It contains no name, no account, no device id, no IP address (the server keeps none in readable form) and no location.
  • It is used only to find and fix bugs, and is kept for 90 days. If the phone is offline, the report waits on the phone and is sent later.
  • Turn it off in the You tab → Send crash reports. Nothing is then kept or sent, and reports still waiting on the phone are not sent.

6. Where the data is kept and who handles it

Account data is stored with Supabase (database and login service), on servers in Singapore. The app and this website reach it only through one function that checks who is asking; the tables themselves cannot be read from outside. To limit abuse, a hashed (not readable) key and a counter are kept for up to 2 days; no IP address is stored in readable form. This website is a plain static site, and downloads and the update check use GitHub, which follows its own privacy statement. We do not sell data, show ads, use third-party analytics or trackers, or share data with anyone else.

7. This website

  • No cookies, no ads, no third-party analytics, scripts or fonts. Everything is calculated in your browser.
  • On the community page, reporting a photo or post needs no account. To count each person once, the browser keeps a random id; the server stores only a scrambled form of it (a hash) with the report.
  • Your browser remembers language, theme, quiz scores and whether you have seen the welcome window, in local storage on your own device. If you log in, a login token is kept there too, until you log out.
  • The first time you open the site, a welcome window offers to create an account or log in. “Continue without account” closes it and the site remembers that choice on your device.
  • A small notice at the bottom of the page can show a note from the Astryl team (you can close it) and, once, ask whether you want to share your approximate place. Your answer is remembered on your device. See “Visitors and places” below.
  • The home page asks the Astryl server for the public counter (accounts, active users, visitors, page views).
  • To work offline and open fast, the website keeps copies of its own pages, scripts and pictures in your browser (a service worker cache). It stores nothing about you and sends nothing. Clearing the site data in your browser removes it; installing the website only adds an icon.
  • The home page shows NASA’s Astronomy Picture of the Day. The text comes through the Astryl server; the picture loads straight from NASA’s server (assets.science.nasa.gov), which sees your IP address like any website. The space station page gets the station’s orbit data from the Astryl server (or, if that fails, from wheretheiss.at). Nothing about you is sent.

Counting page views

To see which pages are read, each page view adds one to a daily total on the Astryl server. This is what is counted, and nothing else:

CountedNot stored at all
The page name, the language, a size class of your screen (small, medium or large), the website that sent you here (if any), and the country.Your IP address itself, a cookie, any id, the time of day, your browser or device model, or anything that could point to one person.

The country is read by the network from the connection when the request arrives; the address itself is not kept. A row only says, for example, “on 9 November, the page planets.html was opened 12 times in English from a medium screen in Armenia”. Rows are kept for 400 days. Nothing is counted if your browser sends “Do Not Track” or “Global Privacy Control”.

Visitors and places

To see how many different people visit, the Astryl server makes a one-way code from your IP address, the day and a secret that only the server knows, and adds that code to the list for the day. The IP address itself is not saved. The code cannot be turned back into an address, and it is different on every day, so a person cannot be followed from one day to the next. The codes are deleted after 90 days. This is not done if your browser sends “Do Not Track” or “Global Privacy Control”.

The place question is optional. If you press “Share”, your browser asks for permission; the point is rounded to about 10 km and added to a count for the day. It carries no code, no account and no IP address, so it cannot be tied to you. If you say no, nothing is sent. Your answer is kept only in your browser.

The team’s notes come from the Astryl server and are the same for everyone; asking for them sends nothing about you.

8. Your choices and rights

  • Delete your account at any time: app → You tab → Account → Delete my account, or on the Account page of this website. Your account and everything stored for it online are removed straight away. The data on your phone stays.
  • Turn any optional switch off at any time. This withdraws your consent for that part; the data it created is removed or stops growing.
  • Log out to stop syncing on a device. Uninstalling the app removes its data from the phone.
  • To ask what is stored about you, to get a copy, or to have something corrected or removed, write to contact@astryl.space and tell us your username. Depending on where you live you may also have the right to complain to your data protection authority.

9. Children

Astryl is for everyone who likes space. If you are under 16, please ask a parent or guardian before making an account. The app is fully usable without one.

10. Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the new version is published on this page and in the app's release notes, and the app asks again before you make a new account. The full source code, including the server code in the folder supabase/, is open for anyone to read.
