What is a black hole?

A place where gravity is so strong that nothing can get back out, not even light.

A black hole is not a hole in space. It is a lot of mass squeezed into a very small volume, so squeezed that its gravity becomes extreme close to it.

The escape speed idea

Every world has an escape speed: how fast you must go to leave it for good. From Earth it is about 11 km per second. Squeeze the same mass into a smaller and smaller ball and the escape speed keeps growing.

A black hole is a ball so small that the escape speed is more than the speed of light, about 300,000 km per second. Nothing can travel faster than light, so nothing that is inside can get out.

Why “black”?

It gives off no light of its own, and light that falls in does not return. What we can see is the glow of hot gas around it and how it pulls on nearby stars.

The point of no return

The edge is called the event horizon. It is not a hard surface you could land on. It is a boundary in space. Outside it, a fast enough rocket can still escape. Inside it, every path leads inward.

Black holes do not suck things in from far away. If the Sun were swapped for a black hole of the same mass, Earth would keep orbiting exactly as it does now, only in the dark and the cold. Gravity far away depends on mass, not on what the mass is made of.

Four words to remember

Event horizon

The boundary of the black hole. Cross it and you cannot come back.

Singularity

The centre, where our theories say the mass is crushed to a point.

Accretion disk

A flat whirl of hot gas that circles the black hole before falling in.

Solar mass

The mass of the Sun. Black holes are measured in “Suns”.
